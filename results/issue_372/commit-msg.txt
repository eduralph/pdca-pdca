fix(progress): sweep surviving child processes after a normal exit

run_with_heartbeat only waited on the direct child; under shell=True (every
gate) that child is just the shell, so anything it backgrounded survived the
call. Measured: one leaked test process burned ~100% of a core for 21 hours,
and a straggler that still holds ports, locks or fixtures when the next run's
gates start in the same worktree is the class of one-off, never-reproducible
gate failure. 228e80b750a1f5595b4d45580baa751d5152ac52 (#368) sessionizes
only bounded children and kills the group only on timeout expiry — a normal
exit, the overwhelmingly common path, was never swept.

On POSIX, every child whose stdio the harness owns (capture / stream_json) or
whose wall clock is bounded now runs in its own session, and however the wait
ends — normal return, timeout, Ctrl-C — surviving group members are swept:
SIGTERM, a short grace, then SIGKILL, with one stderr note naming the swept
command. The sweep runs before the capture streams close, mirroring the
timeout path's kill-then-close order: a straggler holding the capture pipe
otherwise keeps the drain thread blocked mid-read, and closing the stream
then waits on that blocked reader. A clean exit with no survivors prints
nothing and stays byte-identical; interactive calls (no capture, no stream,
no bound) are never sessionized, so they keep the terminal exactly as today.
Non-POSIX behaviour is unchanged.

Known limitation: a swept grandchild is not this process's child, so it
cannot be reaped here — under a non-reaping init (e.g. a minimal container)
the group kill leaves a zombie table entry. The subreaper design is deferred
to #383; a naive waitpid(-1) reaper could steal exit statuses from concurrent
Popen.wait() calls and corrupt a gate verdict — strictly worse than a zombie.
