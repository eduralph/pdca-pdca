fix(plan): stop telling oversized split children to split again

Splitting an oversized bundle inflates the very readout the size
advisory keys on: each child inherits the parent's difficulty, its
dependency tokens, and `Conflicts with:` entries naming its own
siblings — ordering metadata the splitter wrote, not scope the child
acquired. The advisory answered that readout with the same ``consider
`pdca split` first`` the parent was given, at every depth of the
recursion, and the planner and splitter prompts then pointed the next
session at `pdca split` again.

Key the remedy on the honest signal instead:
b4c924d6832bba0450ff39e4c7bf2d8c2d96a112 (#457) made sizing exclude a
child's sibling conflicts from the score and publish their count. When
that count is non-zero before Do, the advisory now names the
provenance — "scores large for a split child (child N of a split of
#X, depth D) — driven by inherited/sibling fields; prefer building
over re-splitting" — and discloses the uncounted number beside the
scored reasons, instead of recommending another split.

The count, not the mere presence of a lineage record, is the
predicate: every child carries lineage forever, so presence would tell
a child whose conflicts are all organic that its size was inherited,
beside its own contradicting conflict count. Keying on the count also
keeps the ordinary remedy reachable on the sizer this project ships —
the stub sizer answers `ok` unconditionally, so gating recovery on the
sizer's own verdict would be dead config on every offline instance.
A child with no sibling conflicts is advised exactly like any other
bundle, and the ordinary advice returns by itself once the siblings
land and their entries leave the brief.

Bundles with no lineage produce byte-identical advisories, and a
bundle that already has a patch keeps its iterate-plan wording. The
same one-sentence provenance note now travels with the planner and
splitter prompts, and the process flowchart documents the new fork.

Fixes #458
