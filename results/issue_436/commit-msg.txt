size-signal: stop charging environment-lost rounds to the slice

The rounds rule — the size backstop's strongest signal (76% precision that a
third round follows) — counted every iteration archive after the last re-plan
boundary without asking what a round was spent on. A round lost to an
environment fault (a stale host CLI, an absent oracle, a sandbox that cannot
bind loopback) was charged to the slice like any other. Observed downstream: a
66 KB / 4-file patch fired the backstop on the rounds rule alone and the
sign-off item recommended splitting the slice, when at least one round had
demonstrably been burned by a too-old cargo-deny; the human overrode it twice.
That is churn evidence about the host presented as evidence about the slice —
a measurement bug, not a threshold one, so no retune can fix it.

`iteration_rounds` now opens each counted archive's own evidence (the
per-round check-gates.json and review record the driver archives with the
attempt) and excludes a round only when an environment fault was the SOLE
recorded driver of that iterate: (a) no plain gating fail, (b) at least one
gating row recorded `unverifiable` or bearing a truthy `flaky` key — the
consumer side of the #371 confirm-once contract, dormant until its recorder
lands — and (c) an archived review that drove nothing of its own. Mixed-cause
rounds (an environmental row alongside a real finding), plain fails, and
all-green reviewer-driven rounds still count. Ambiguous, missing, or
unreadable evidence counts the round: over-counting keeps the backstop, and
silent shrinkage is the failure mode the recorded signal already refuses.

This extends the boundary f616bc9cedef34faeeac9dc334fa19d8008bab99 landed
(rounds attributable to the CURRENT brief) with a second one: attributable to
the slice, not the host. Because scripts/size-calibrate imports the same
`iteration_rounds`, the miner inherits the attribution with no second
implementation — the runtime and the calibration corpus measure one quantity.

Fixes #436
