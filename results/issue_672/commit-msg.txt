flow: keep a fold-skipped bundle's commits off the carried line

When a re-issued stack-mode run carries bundles finished in an earlier run,
it folds them onto one integration line and pushes it once. A bundle whose
merge fails in that fold (a conflict, a deleted branch, any merge error) is
skipped, but until now only that bundle was left out. Another finished
bundle built on it, or holding any of its commits, was still merged and
pushed, so the skipped bundle's work reached the line anyway and the next
wave was built on top of it. The docs listed this as a known gap.

The carry's fold now works out each skipped bundle's commits (those on its
branch or its PR head that neither the base nor the line's starting commit
has) and leaves out every finished bundle whose head reaches any of them,
whether it was merged before the skip or after, with a `Depends on` edge or
without. Bundles holding commits of one left out this way are left out too.
If anything merged has to come off, the line is rebuilt from the same
starting commit with only the bundles kept, so there is still exactly one
push and nothing is pushed and then rewritten. Each bundle left out is
reported with the skipped bundle as its cause ("was built on ..., which is
not carried"), and its `Depends on` dependents wait, as for any bundle not
carried. A bundle that fails on its own and also holds an earlier failed
bundle's commits is reported for the latter.

The hold is on purpose wider than strictly needed: a bundle sharing a commit
with a skipped one is held even when that commit is its own. On a line
continued from an earlier run, commits of the skipped bundle that the
earlier run already pushed stay on it; this change adds none. Both are
written in docs/07-crosscutting.md. In-run folds are unchanged and do no
extra work.

Fixes #672
